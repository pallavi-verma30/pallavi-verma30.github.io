Coverage up, newborn deaths down

How this was built & what it can't tell you

The national series come from WHO/UNICEF estimates (DTP3, measles) and UN IGME (neonatal mortality). The 2018 state figures were rebuilt from district-level reports: I loaded the raw files into DuckDB, cleaned out impossible and missing values, then aggregated coverage by summing doses and eligible population per state (a population-weighted rate, not a simple average of districts).

Limitations, stated plainly: the national trend is country-level while the state view is a single year (2018), so they sit at different scales. Coverage versus mortality is an association, not causation. And the administrative data is noisy — beyond the 225 impossible measles records, the polio figures were too inconsistent (third-dose records exceeding first-dose in most states) to compute a reliable dropout rate, so that indicator was left out rather than shown misleadingly.
